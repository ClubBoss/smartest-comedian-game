## Acceptance philosophy

PASS does **not** mean every question is equally difficult. The target is a good comedy-game solve curve:

1. The prompt does not hand over a dominant answer immediately.
2. There are multiple plausible wrong branches worth saying out loud.
3. Hint 1 points without killing the question.
4. Hint 2 makes the core answer reasonably reachable by reasoning.
5. Hint 3 is a near-solve, preventing dead air.
6. The answer is short enough for a host to judge without litigation.
7. The reveal remains funny, absurd, surprising or satisfying.
8. We reject further cleverness when it would add trivia specificity without material show value.

Probabilities below are editorial estimates, not measured human response rates.

## Per-card acceptance notes

### A1 — Elmer McCurdy — PASS

The earlier version asking directly “who did they pretend to be?” over-signaled family. V2.2 asks **how carnival men obtained a body for free after the undertaker had refused carnival buyers**, creating real branches: theft, bribery, forged order, police authority, relatives, debt, burial promise. H2 makes “family right” reachable, H3 closes it. No gain from further obscuring.

### A2 — Spider gift — PASS

The earlier “why wrap a fake gift?” almost answered itself with “delay discovery.” V2.2 asks for the **cost of cheating after mating already begins**. This preserves the funny sexual deception but prevents the one-step answer. H1/H2 provide a fair causal path: post-start penalty -> female controls duration -> shorter mating. Strong improvement without technical trivia lock.

### A3 — Groom of the Stool — PASS

Prompt has wide branches (food, dressing, bathing, medicine, bed, grooming, toilet). H1 removes obvious servant categories, H2 points toward a private bodily moment, H3 near-solves via `Stool`. The exact answer is funny and short. Further cue removal would make it arbitrary; further cue addition would make it obvious.

### A4 — Bowerbird — PASS

Cold solve requires inventing a visual purpose from object ordering, but H1 and especially H2 make forced perspective reasonably derivable. Accept policy does not require the English art term. This is the correct kind of “hard cold, easy with hints” question. Do not make the player name a scientific mechanism more precisely than “optical illusion / perspective.”

### A5 — Berners Street — PASS

The old logistics question was reflex-easy because `1810 + many people arrive` implies letters. The wager angle keeps the same absurd event and is not obscure in play because H2 explicitly moves the search toward **fame of the address**. Cold guess-space is excellent: revenge, bet on traffic, public nuisance, publicity stunt, social experiment, notoriety, attention. This is not knowledge-locked after H2.

### A6 — Apollo covers — PASS

Good benchmark card. Cold branches include souvenirs, authentication, postal ritual, collectors, publicity, family keepsake, insurance. H2 makes financial insurance a legitimate deduction; H3 provides the resale mechanism. Super slot is justified without becoming niche.

### B1 — Garlic — PASS

One of the strongest cards. Cold branches are broad and naturally funny. H1 rules out inspecting the garlic, H2 moves the result upward, H3 makes the historical pseudo-medical model obvious. Excellent curve and payoff.

### B2 — Wife sale — PASS

Removing the explicit “illegal sale” explanation from the core and adding the pre-known buyer/symbolic price keeps the story simple but prevents an instant one-line “because divorce was hard” from feeling fully sufficient. H2 then makes the intended social-divorce function reachable. Do not add detailed lover-liability law: that was over-engineering.

### B3 — Cardiff Giant — PASS / DELIBERATE RELIEF

This remains the easiest card in Qualifier B. That is intentional. The fake-of-a-fake reveal is visually strong and extremely satisfying; making the angle more obscure harmed show value. A player may solve it cold, but the pack can absorb one 25–35% relief card. Only real human evidence of repeated immediate solves before the host finishes the question should reopen it.

### B4 — Flatworm fencing — PASS

Without the hermaphrodite hint, the prompt allows dominance, injury avoidance, mate competition, sperm placement, territorial behavior and other branches. H1 introduces the key biology, H2 turns it into a role conflict, H3 nearly states donor-vs-recipient. Adult/comic payoff is high, scoring is clear, and we avoid the inaccurate simplification “loser becomes female.”

### B5 — Poison Squad — PASS

Government chemistry lab + monitored meals creates many experimental variables. The prompt no longer says “preservatives” before asking what changed. H2 (`Poison Squad`) points toward harmful additives; H3 gives storage chemicals. Good medium card.

### B6 — Princess Caraboo — PASS

The prior ladder was slightly trivia-locked: “someone who knew her” did not make `landlady` sufficiently inferable. V2.2 fixes this by making H2 reveal the housing relationship. Cold remains very broad, H1 eliminates experts, H2 produces the aha moment, H3 closes. This is now a fair Super rather than a know-it-or-don't card.

### F1 — Cuttlefish — PASS

The old prompt explicitly named female + rival, making the double display almost self-solving. V2.2 asks for both the **trio configuration** and the two signals. H1 gives competing social goals; H2 supplies the actors; H3 supplies the exact displays. Strong final opener: accessible after hints, not reflexive cold.

### F2 — Cadaver Synod — PASS

The scene is vivid enough to generate several explanations (ritual, humiliation, legitimacy, reburial, trial). “Court” is reachable but not forced from the prompt alone. H2 makes it easy enough, which is exactly the desired profile. The proposed three-finger variant was rejected as needless trivia specificity.

### F3 — Great Stork Derby — PASS

Cold prompt supports inheritance contest, civic competition, population goal, charity, births and other theories. H1 reveals women, H2 moves to maternity events, and a good player should infer births before H3. Requiring both counted metric and prize makes Team scoring meaningful without being pedantic.

### F4 — Roland the Farter — PASS

Best pure comedy guess-space in the bank. Cold exact solve is intentionally tiny. Crucially, it is no longer trivia-locked: H2 gives jump + whistle and asks only for the third simultaneous action; H3 makes the body-end joke near-explicit. This is a model for “impossible cold, fair after hints.”

### F5 — Radithor — PASS

Removing the brand name `Radithor` from the spoken prompt removes the strongest radiation cue while preserving the original lead-coffin payoff. Cold branches now include biohazard, grave robbery, preservation, poison and radiation. H2 says the hazard comes from the body; H3 gives radium. No need to switch to the jaw-surgery detail.

### F6 — Thérèse Humbert — PASS

Asking for the **mechanism that kept invisible wealth believable** is materially better than asking what was found in the safe. H1 defines the explanatory problem, H2 gives the inheritance dispute, H3 names fictitious heirs/courts. Some players may say “fake lawsuit” early; that is legitimate reasoning, not a wording leak.

### F7 — Poyais — PASS

The old prompt `people sailed to a promoted country — what was wrong?` made “country was fake” too obvious. V2.2 introduces the useful nuance that the physical territory existed and asks what existed only on paper. This creates branches (ownership, resources, government, city, infrastructure, treaties) while preserving the original reveal. H2/H3 converge cleanly. No replacement with Victor Lustig is warranted.

### F8 — Brinkley — PASS

Delayed A/B/C solves the random-guess problem. Cold stage is open-ended and funny; H1 gives “animal donor” without naming species; H2 then safely introduces three plausible animal-tissue options; H3 closes with goats. This is a good final mechanic because the question becomes easier exactly when the show needs closure.

## Diminishing-returns gate

Further desk-editing is now more likely to damage simplicity, natural speech or payoff than to create a meaningful improvement.

Therefore:

- `20 / 20 = EDITORIAL PASS`
- `REFLEX_CRITICAL = 0`
- `TRIVIA_LOCKED_AFTER_H2 = 0`
- `AMBIGUOUS_SCORING_CRITICAL = 0`
- `REPLACE_REQUIRED = 0`
- `FURTHER_DESK_REWRITE = STOP`

The next meaningful evidence is **human blind testing**, not another language-model rewrite loop. A future card should reopen only if real play shows one of these failure modes:

1. repeated correct buzz before the host finishes the prompt;
2. nobody can reach the core answer after H2;
3. host cannot judge plausible answers consistently;
4. reveal consistently lands flat;
5. one pack produces a materially faster/slower solve cadence than the other.

This acceptance does not authorize production sync or deployment; it only closes the desk-based difficulty/editorial gauntlet.
